# Evidence

## The figures

Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index, built on more than 40 million licences and $75 billion of tracked spend, found:

- Organizations leave an average of **36% of their SaaS licences unused**.
- Median SaaS spend is **$9,455 per employee**.
- AI-native tool spend is up **393% year on year** in organizations with more than 10,000 employees.

Published 29 January 2026. ([Zylo, 2026 SaaS Management Index](https://zylo.com/news/2026-saas-management-index))

## How to use this, and how not to

**Use it to explain why the audit is worth running**, in one line if the user asks. It is aggregate industry data from a vendor that manages SaaS spend, not a claim about this user's own stack.

**Do not present 36% as a prediction for this user's result.** Their actual unused share could be higher or lower. Run the audit and report what it finds, not what the industry average suggests it should find.

**Do not extrapolate the enterprise AI-native growth figure to a freelancer or small team.** That number describes organizations above 10,000 employees. A solo user's AI tool spend has no comparable benchmark in this source.

**Do not quote a dollar figure as this user's waste.** $9,455 per employee is a median across the index's dataset, not evidence about anyone using this skill. If a dollar total is useful, it is the one Step 5 calculates from the user's own rows, never a borrowed industry number.

## What this does not establish

The index says licences go unused. It does not say why, and it does not distinguish a genuinely wasted subscription from one correctly kept for low-frequency use, such as a disaster-recovery tool. That distinction is Step 4's job, made from the user's own answer, not from this source.
