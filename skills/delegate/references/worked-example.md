# Worked example

One piece of work taken through all six steps. The company is invented.

---

## The request

A freelance operations consultant has a client, Merribel Foods, an invented forty-person food distributor. The contract includes a quarterly review document: what changed in their fulfilment numbers, what it means, and what to do next quarter. It runs to about twelve pages, it takes most of three days, and the consultant has done five of them.

They ask: how much of this can the agent do.

---

## Step 1: Name the work and set the bar

**Output:** a twelve-page quarterly review document.

**Who sees it:** the client's operations director and, for the last two quarters, their managing director. It goes out under the consultant's name and it is the visible artefact of a retainer worth more than the document.

**If it is wrong and nobody notices:** a wrong number in the fulfilment analysis becomes a decision about warehouse staffing. It would surface a quarter later, as a bad outcome nobody traces back to this document. A weak recommendation is recoverable. A wrong figure is not.

That last line sets the bar. **Numbers get checked to source. Prose gets read for sense.** Two different standards in one document, and the split matters more than any other decision here.

---

## Step 2: Split at the seams

Chronologically this is: pull the data, analyse it, write it, format it. That split is useless, because "analyse it" is where all the judgement lives and it is still one lump.

Split by kind of thinking instead, and it comes apart into seven parts.

1. Pull the fulfilment figures from the client's exports for the quarter
2. Reconcile them against last quarter's document, so the comparison is like for like
3. Calculate the movements and build the tables
4. Decide which three movements matter enough to lead on
5. Explain why each of the three happened
6. Recommend what to do next quarter
7. Write and format the document

---

## Step 3: Allocate

| Part | Test that decided it | Goes to |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Pull figures | Correct is well defined: the numbers match the export | Model |
| 2. Reconcile to last quarter | Correct is well defined, and the previous document is available as the reference | Model |
| 3. Calculate and build tables | Correct is well defined and checkable by re-adding | Model |
| 4. Decide the three that matter | Needs context never written down: which of these the operations director already worries about, and what they were told last quarter | Consultant |
| 5. Explain why | Depends on part 4, and on a conversation with the warehouse manager that is not in any file | Consultant |
| 6. Recommend | Highest consequence, lowest recoverability, and the reason the retainer exists | Consultant |
| 7. Write and format | Correct is well defined once parts 4 to 6 are decided. Test 4 applies, since writing these is part of how the consultant thinks, so the draft is delegated and the argument is not | Model, from the consultant's outline |

Part 5 is the interesting one. It looks like analysis a model could do, and the reason it stays is not capability. It is that the true explanation for one of the three movements is usually something a person said in a call, and a plausible explanation that fits the data is worse than no explanation, because it is persuasive and wrong.

---

## Step 4: The checks

| Part | The check | Take it back if | Skipping costs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Pull figures | Spot-check three figures against the export, including the largest and the most awkward category | Any mismatch at all | A wrong number becomes a staffing decision |
| 2. Reconcile | Confirm the category names match last quarter's document one for one, and that any renamed category is flagged rather than silently mapped | Any silent mapping | A comparison that is not like for like, which is invisible in the output |
| 3. Calculate and tables | Re-add each column. Confirm every total equals the sum of its rows and that percentages are against the stated base | Two errors, or any error in a total | Same as part 1 |
| 7. Draft from outline | Read against the outline for drift. Search the document for the previous client's name and for any bracket left in | Any argument in the draft that is not in the outline | A document that reads well and says something the consultant did not decide |

The check on part 2 is the one experience added. On the third quarterly review, a category was renamed by the client and mapped to the nearest match without comment, and the comparison was wrong for a whole section. Nothing in the output looked wrong. That is why the check is stated as "flagged rather than silently mapped" and not as "check the categories".

Part 3 is worth pausing on for the cost rule. Re-adding the tables takes about fifteen minutes. Building them takes about ninety. The check is worth it. Had the numbers been spread over forty tables instead of six, the check would have cost more than the work, and the right answer would have been to keep part 3 or to change the document.

---

## Step 5: It recurs

**How often:** quarterly.

**Are the inputs stable:** mostly. The export format has changed once in five quarters, without notice.

**What drift looks like:** the export gains or renames a column, and parts 1 to 3 keep running and produce something subtly wrong. This is exactly the failure the part 2 check exists for, which is the argument for keeping a person on that check rather than automating it away.

**Review on:** the first working day of the quarter, before the next document starts.

Parts 1 to 3 are candidates for running unattended in future. Not yet, on the strength of one unannounced format change in five quarters. Two clean quarters with the check in place, then reconsider.

---

## What the consultant actually got

Three days of work became about a day and a half, and the day and a half that remains is parts 4 to 6, which is the part the client is paying for.

Two things came out of the process that were not the point of it. The reconciliation check in part 2 is now a step in the work whoever does it, because writing it down was what made the earlier failure visible. And the consultant noticed that part 6 has never had a check at all, in five quarters, because it stays with them. That is a finding for a different skill, and it went in the "could not decide" section of the table.
