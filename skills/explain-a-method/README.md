# explain a method

**Understand a productivity method in a minute: what it is, how to start in ten minutes, whether it holds up, and when it fails.**

Productivity content explains methods with confidence and leaves out the part that matters: when they do not work. Some of it describes methods nobody can trace. This skill explains one method or compares two with the same six parts every time: what it is, a 10-minute start, a worked example from your field, an honest evidence label with one sentence, when it backfires, and a step to try today. If it cannot recognise the method, it says so instead of inventing one.

The failure this exists to prevent: **confident explanations of methods that omit when they fail, or invented methods.**

**Reach for it:** when you hear a method named and want to know how it works, whether it is worth it, or how it differs from another. Part of [work-design](https://github.com/theAnirudhKumar/work-design).

---

## Example

*Illustrative. The person is invented.*

You type: `How does time blocking work, and is it worth it? I'm a secondary school teacher.`

You get (shortened):

```
Time Blocking

What it is: you assign tasks to specific blocks of your calendar
instead of keeping an open list.

Start in 10 minutes:
1. List tomorrow's three most important tasks.
2. Look at your free periods. Put each task in one, with a start time.
3. Add a 15-minute block for marking overflow.
4. Leave one block empty for the unexpected.

Example (illustrative): Priya, a teacher, blocks period 4 for planning,
the 3:30 slot for emails, and nothing before first lesson.

Evidence: Practice only. Widely used, little direct testing.

When it backfires: when your day is set by a bell or other people, and
the blocks collapse by 10am; when you plan every minute and feel behind.

Try it today: block one free period for the task you keep avoiding.
Set a phone alarm for the start.
```

## What it does

| Part | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| What it is | Two sentences, no hype |
| Start in 10 minutes | Three to five steps you can do now |
| Worked example | From your field, with an invented person |
| Evidence | Strong, Some, Mixed or Practice only, plus one sentence |
| When it backfires | At least two real situations |
| Try it today | One action under 15 minutes |

For "X vs Y" you get a small comparison table and a line that picks by situation.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** the name of a method.

**Better with:** your field and situation, so the example fits.

**Best with:** the method library from `find-my-method`. Without it, the skill works from general knowledge and says so on the evidence label.

It cannot keep time, so where a timer is needed it tells you to set one.

## Where it hands off

- You want a recommendation, not an explanation: `find-my-method`
- You want it applied to today: `plan-my-day`
- You want a full setup: `build-my-system`

---

## What this does not do

- **Not a recommendation.** It explains; `find-my-method` chooses.
- **Not a study summary.** It cites no study or percentage it cannot verify.
- **Not medical advice.** Sleep, exercise and rest methods are general wellbeing practice. Follow a professional's advice for any condition.
- **Not an inventor.** If a method is not recognised, it says so.
