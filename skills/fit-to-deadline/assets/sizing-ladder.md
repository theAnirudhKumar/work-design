## Filled example

*Illustrative. The project and people are invented.*

**The work:** a pricing review for Harlow Print, a small print shop.
**Outcome:** the owner can choose a new price list at the Friday meeting, knowing what it does to margin on her five biggest jobs.
**Time available:** 9 working hours (Wed afternoon, all of Thu; Friday morning is the meeting). Assumed: 4 focused hours Wednesday, 5 on Thursday.

| Part | Hours (guess) | Contribution | Depends on it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pull cost and price for the five biggest jobs | 2 | essential | the margin table |
| Margin table before and after a price change | 2 | essential | the recommendation |
| Compare with three nearby shops' public prices | 2 | helps | the recommendation |
| Customer segment breakdown | 3 | polish | none |
| Two-page write-up with a recommendation | 2 | essential | none |
| Formatting and charts | 1 | polish | none |

| Version | What is in it | Hours | Against the budget |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Minimum** | Five-job table, margin table, write-up | 6 | 3 under |
| **Target** | Minimum plus the three-shop comparison | 8 | 1 under |
| **Stretch** | Formatting and charts, then segment breakdown | 9 to 12 | Only the formatting fits |

**Why the Minimum still counts:** the outcome is a choice informed by margin on the five biggest jobs. The table and the margin change deliver exactly that. The comparison makes the choice better informed but does not make it possible.

| # | Cut | Hours saved | What it costs | Must also cut |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Customer segment breakdown | 3 | Nobody at the meeting will miss it; the owner did not ask for it | None |
| 2 | Formatting and charts | 1 | The tables look plain; the numbers are the same | None |
| 3 | Three-shop comparison | 2 | The owner will not know whether the new prices are in line with nearby shops, and may ask | The comparison line in the write-up |

**Midpoint rule:** At Thursday 09:00, if the margin table is not finished, cut the three-shop comparison and work to the Minimum.

**If the Minimum is too thin:** the owner asks "but is this in line with what others charge?" and there is no answer. Cheap check: on Wednesday evening, send the five-job table to the owner and ask "is this the right five jobs?" If she names a different job, replace it before building on it.

**What I had to assume:** the five biggest jobs are the right basis (changes the whole ladder); cost figures exist and are findable in two hours (not checked); the hours per day.
