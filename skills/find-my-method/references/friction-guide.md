# Friction guide

A lookup from the ten commonest frictions to the 2 to 4 methods that address each. First method listed is the usual primary; the others are backups or pairs. Names follow the library. Before recommending, find the name in `method-index.md` and read its card. If a name below is not in the index, use the closest card by its "Best for" line and say you substituted. If the library is absent, work from general knowledge and mark labels accordingly.

Pick by the person's constraint, not the order of the list. Change at most two things at once.

---

## 1. Can't start

Tells: "I keep putting it off", "I stare at it", "I don't know where to begin".
- **Five-minute rule** when the task is dreaded but probably less painful than it feels.
- **Tiny first step** when the task feels too big or vague to begin.
- **Eat the frog** when one dreaded task sets the tone for the day.
- **Pomodoro Technique** when a short fixed start lowers the bar. Skip when the work needs long unbroken stretches. For one stuck task, also consider `get-unstuck`.

## 2. Too many things at once

Tells: "everything is open", "I switch all day", "ten projects".
- **Ivy Lee method** for a person who can order six tasks each evening and follow the order.
- **1-3-5 rule** when the day is mixed sizes and a cap helps.
- **Personal Kanban** when the work is ongoing projects and a work-in-progress limit is the fix.
- **Monotasking** as the free option when nothing else sticks.

## 3. Constantly distracted

Tells: "I check my phone", "I lose an hour online", "I can't concentrate".
- **Pomodoro Technique** when distraction is internal and short sprints help.
- **Distraction parking lot** for the urge to check something mid-task.
- **Phone-away rule** when the phone is the main pull.
- **Deep work** when the person controls a stretch of the day.
If the distraction comes from other people, see friction 7 and `protect-my-focus`. For a session now, `start-a-focus-session`.

## 4. Overwhelmed by the list

Tells: "too much to do", "the list never shrinks", "I don't know what matters".
- **Brain dump** first, when the list is in the head and nowhere else.
- **Eisenhower matrix** when urgent items crowd out important ones.
- **Ivy Lee method** when the choice itself is exhausting.
- **MoSCoW** when there is a fixed deadline and too much scope. For a pasted list, `sort-my-tasks`.

## 5. Never enough time

Tells: "no time", "I'm always behind", "the day disappears".
- **Time audit** first, when nobody knows where the hours go.
- **Time blocking** when the person controls their calendar.
- **Not-to-do list** when the answer is fewer commitments, not better scheduling.
- **Parkinson's-law deadlines** when tasks swell to fill whatever time exists.

## 6. Forgets things

Tells: "it slipped my mind", "I missed the deadline I knew about", "I keep it in my head".
- **Brain dump** first, to get everything out of the head.
- **Waiting-for list** when the loss is things handed to others.
- **Weekly review** to catch what capture misses.
- **Getting Things Done** when the volume is high and a full capture-and-review loop is wanted. High setup cost.

## 7. Meetings and interruptions eat the day

Tells: "back to back", "Slack never stops", "I get nothing done until evening".
- **Meeting-free blocks** when the person can protect time on the calendar.
- **Task batching** when interruptions are constant and work must happen in gaps.
- **Notification batching** when the pull is pings and messages.
- **Maker's schedule** when the work needs long stretches to create.
Hand off to `protect-my-focus` for the full audit and scripts.

## 8. Can't stick to habits

Tells: "I start strong and stop", "I miss two days and quit".
- **Tiny Habits** when the habit is too big for a bad day.
- **Habit stacking** when an anchor already exists.
- **Two-day rule** when one miss turns into quitting.
- **Don't break the chain** only for people who find a streak motivating; it backfires on perfectionists. Hand off to `build-a-habit` for the full design.

## 9. Goals feel vague

Tells: "I want to be better at", "no direction", "busy but going nowhere".
- **Goal ladder** when days feel disconnected from any bigger aim.
- **SMART goals** for a quick sharpening of one goal.
- **WOOP** when the goal is clear but obstacles keep winning.
- **Milestone mapping** when the goal is a long project with a date.

## 10. Tired and burnt out in a general sense

Tells: "I'm exhausted", "I have no energy", "I'm running on fumes".
- **Energy audit** first, to find when energy fades and why.
- **Prime-time scheduling** to put the hardest work in the best hours.
- **Shutdown ritual** and **Rest day** to protect recovery.
Do not add productivity methods to a person who needs rest. This is general wellbeing practice, not medical advice. If it is severe or persistent, say once that a professional who can help is worth talking to, and continue the practical part only if they want it. For the wider picture, see `balance-my-life`.

---

## When two frictions show up

Pick the one that blocks the other. "Forgets things" and "overwhelmed" usually start with capture. "Can't start" and "distracted" usually start with a short fixed session. "Burnt out" outranks everything: start with rest and fewer commitments.
