# Writing it up

For when the output is going somewhere other than the reader's own notes: an article, a review, a message to a team, a recommendation to a manager.

## Tone

**A knowledgeable friend, not a warning label.** The reader is interested but not committed. That is exactly the moment to say: before I show you the good part, here is what I wish I had known.

What it should not sound like:

- A section that scares people off a good tool
- A list of negatives with no context and no resolution
- General moralising about privacy or data practices
- Speculation. Only documented or confirmed things belong here

The test: would you say this out loud to someone who already loves the tool? If the answer is no because it is unfair rather than because it is unwelcome, rewrite it.

## Placement

Put the findings **after the reader knows what the tool is and why it exists, and before the walkthrough of what it does.** Too early and it reads as a hit piece on something the reader cannot picture yet. Too late and they have already decided.

## Openings that work

- "Before we get into features, here is what most people do not find out until later."
- "A few things worth flagging. Not dealbreakers for most people, but worth knowing."
- "The thing that went viral about this tool, and what actually matters."
- "There are a few things buried in the docs that are worth knowing before you install."

## Structure

Bold the finding, explain it in two or three sentences, say what it means for the reader. Two to four findings. More than four and the reader stops reading, which means the important one goes unread.

Close the section by handing the tool back:

> None of that makes it a bad product. It makes it an early one. Here is what it actually does.

Or, where the call is stop, say so and say why in the same breath, then keep describing the tool honestly. A reader who disagrees with the call still deserves an accurate picture.

## Two habits worth keeping

**Quote, do not characterise.** "The terms grant a licence to reproduce and modify uploaded content" beats "the terms are aggressive". The reader can weigh a quote.

**Publish what you could not check.** Naming the gaps is what makes the rest credible, and it is the thing almost no review does.
