# Worked examples

Four filled cards from unrelated fields. All people, products and organisations are invented. Use them to calibrate how specific each line should be, not as text to copy.

---

## 1. Marketing launch (full card)

**Work card: launch the Fernlight desk lamp**

*Depth: full. A multi-week piece of work, so this card covers the first stretch (until the launch email goes out).*

**1. Outcome**
By 14 March, the Fernlight lamp page is live, the launch email has gone to the existing list, and every order reaches the fulfilment inbox.

**2. Who it is for, and what they will do with it**
The existing email list (about 4,000 past buyers, assumed). They will open one email and either buy or not. The founder will use the order data on 15 March to decide whether to run paid ads.

**3. Why now**
The first stock batch arrives on 10 March, and storage costs start then. Waiting a month costs a month of storage.

**4. Appetite**
Up to 25 working hours across three weeks, because the launch is worth one batch of stock and no more. At the limit: send what exists, skip paid ads.

**5. Done looks like**
- The product page is live and a test order completes end to end.
- One email is scheduled for 14 March 09:00, with the link checked on a phone.
- A test order arrives in the fulfilment inbox with address and item correct.

Excellent: the email reads like a note from the maker, and the first reply from a customer gets an answer within the hour.

**6. Out of scope**
- Paid ads and influencer outreach
- A second colour, bundles, or a discount code scheme
- Redesigning the rest of the shop

**7. Biggest risk, and the tripwire**
The photos. Without good ones the page and the email both stall, and shooting them depends on stock arriving.
If the lamp is not in hand by 10 March, then shoot with the prototype and note "final finish may vary" on the page.

**8. Who or what helps**
| Part | Who | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Email draft | AI draft, founder edits | Founder checks every claim and price against the product sheet |
| Photos | Ask the freelance photographer for a half-day | Book this week |
| Test order | Alone | Cannot be handed over |

**9. First move**
Today, 20 minutes: write the three done criteria in the shared doc and message the photographer asking for the earliest half-day before 10 March.

**10. What I had to assume**
- The list is about 4,000 and mostly past buyers. This changes the appetite if wrong.
- Stock arrives 10 March as stated in the message. This changes the tripwire date.
- No paid launch budget exists. This changes out of scope.
- Could not check: whether the shop platform supports scheduled sends.

**Questions**
1. Is there a hard date from a retailer or event? (changes line 4)

## 2. Lab experiment (full card)

**Work card: pilot of the buffer-stability test**

*Depth: full.*

**1. Outcome**
By Friday 22nd, we know whether Buffer B holds its pH within 0.2 units over seven days at room temperature, from three replicate runs.

**2. Who it is for, and what they will do with it**
The PI, who will decide on Monday whether to adopt Buffer B for the whole assay series. Also the lab manager, who needs to know whether to order more of it.

**3. Why now**
The supplier's price break ends on the 30th, and the next assay series starts the week after.

**4. Appetite**
Up to 6 bench hours plus seven days of waiting, because this is a go/no-go on a purchase, not a publishable result. At the limit: report the replicates run so far with the gap stated.

**5. Done looks like**
- Three replicates started on the same day with the same lot of Buffer B.
- pH recorded at day 0, 3 and 7 for each, in the shared sheet, with the meter calibration date.
- A two-line summary: pass or fail against the 0.2 limit, and the spread across replicates.

Excellent: the PI can read the summary without asking which lot, which meter, or what temperature.

**6. Out of scope**
- Testing other buffers or temperatures
- Any statistics beyond range across replicates
- Writing this up as a protocol

**7. Biggest risk, and the tripwire**
The pH meter drifting during the week and being mistaken for buffer drift.
If the meter fails its two-point check on day 3, then recalibrate, re-read day 3 samples, and note it in the sheet before going on.

**8. Who or what helps**
| Part | Who | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Setup and readings | Alone | Needs hands on the bench |
| Meter calibration check | Ask the lab manager for the calibration log | One question |
| Summary table | AI can format the sheet | Check every number against the raw readings |

**9. First move**
Tomorrow at 9:00, 25 minutes: label three tubes with lot number and date, and write the day 0, 3 and 7 slots in the sheet.

**10. What I had to assume**
- The 0.2 limit is the PI's real threshold. This changes the done criteria.
- Room temperature is stable enough. Not checked.
- One lot of Buffer B is enough for three replicates.

**Questions**
1. Is 0.2 the real threshold? (changes line 5)

## 3. Legal memo (full card)

**Work card: memo on whether the supplier can terminate for convenience**

*Depth: full.*

**1. Outcome**
By Thursday noon, the general counsel has a memo of two pages or fewer answering whether the supplier can end the agreement without cause, with a recommendation on what to ask for in renegotiation.

**2. Who it is for, and what they will do with it**
The general counsel, who will brief the commercial lead on Friday and decide whether to open renegotiation.

**3. Why now**
The supplier has hinted at a price change. The renewal notice window closes in six weeks.

**4. Appetite**
Up to 8 hours, because it is a first-view memo for an internal decision, not an opinion for an outside party. At the limit: send what exists with open points labelled.

**5. Done looks like**
- The answer is in the first paragraph: yes, no, or depends on named conditions.
- Every conclusion cites the clause number it rests on.
- Open questions that need outside counsel are listed separately and not guessed at.

Excellent: the commercial lead could read the first half page and know what to ask for.

**6. Out of scope**
- Other agreements with the same supplier
- Jurisdictions other than the one named in the governing law clause
- Drafting replacement clauses

**7. Biggest risk, and the tripwire**
An amendment or side letter that changes the termination clause and is not in the file.
If no one confirms by Wednesday 10:00 that the file is complete, then the memo states that it assumes the file is complete, in the first paragraph.

**8. Who or what helps**
| Part | Who | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Find all amendments | Ask the contracts administrator | One question |
| First read and clause map | AI | Every clause cited must be opened and read by the author |
| Conclusion | Alone | Judgement and responsibility stay with the author |

**9. First move**
This afternoon, 20 minutes: email the contracts administrator asking for every amendment, side letter and notice to this supplier, with a Wednesday 10:00 deadline.

**10. What I had to assume**
- One governing law applies. Changes line 6.
- The renewal date is as stated by the user. Not checked against the document.
- Could not check: the contract text itself, which was not provided.

**Questions**
1. Has anyone already given the commercial lead a view? (changes line 1)

## 4. Wedding (full card)

**Work card: Priya and Marcus's wedding, the first stretch (venue and date)**

*Depth: full, for the first stretch only. Each later stretch gets its own card.*

**1. Outcome**
By 30 April, one venue and one date are booked with a deposit paid, and a guest list of no more than 80 is agreed.

**2. Who it is for, and what they will do with it**
The two of them, and both sets of parents, who will use the date to book travel and time off.

**3. Why now**
Venues in the preferred season are booking up, and relatives have asked for a date to arrange travel.

**4. Appetite**
Up to 12 hours over four weeks, plus two venue visits, because this decision shapes everything after it. At the limit: choose the best of the venues already seen.

**5. Done looks like**
- A signed booking and a receipt for the deposit.
- A written budget ceiling both agree to, with the venue cost inside it.
- A guest list in a shared sheet with a count, signed off by both.

Excellent: both sets of parents say they are happy about the date without being asked.

**6. Out of scope**
- Catering, photographer, flowers and outfits
- Honeymoon
- Registry

**7. Biggest risk, and the tripwire**
Disagreement on the guest count, which drives every venue price.
If the two cannot agree a number by 12 April, then they pick the larger venue's capacity as the ceiling and cut the list to fit afterwards.

**8. Who or what helps**
| Part | Who | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shortlist venues | AI draft from criteria | Check availability and prices directly with each venue |
| Visits | Both, together | Cannot be handed over |
| Guest list | Each parent supplies a draft | Ask by 5 April |

**9. First move**
Tonight, 20 minutes: each writes a guest list number on paper, separately, then compares.

**10. What I had to assume**
- A season preference and a rough budget exist but were not given. Changes lines 4 and 5.
- A venue is needed rather than a private home. Changes lines 1 and 6.

**Questions**
1. What is the budget ceiling? (changes lines 4 and 5)

## 5. Thesis chapter (5-line card)

**Work card: Chapter 3 first draft**

```
Outcome:    A complete first draft of Chapter 3 is with my supervisor by Friday 17:00.
Done:       Every section has text, even rough; every figure referenced in the text
            exists; the argument can be stated in three sentences at the chapter's start.
Out:        Polishing prose, the literature review update, the appendix tables.
First move: Tomorrow 9:00, 30 minutes: write the three-sentence argument at the top of
            the file and list the section headings under it.
Assumed:    The supervisor wants a full draft rather than an outline (changes Outcome).
            The data for the two figures is final (not checked).
```

## 6. Software feature (5-line card)

**Work card: CSV export on the reports page**

```
Outcome:    A signed-in user can download the current report as a CSV from the reports
            page, in production, by Thursday.
Done:       The file opens in a spreadsheet with correct columns; a report of 50,000
            rows downloads without timing out; the button is hidden for users without
            report access.
Out:        Scheduled exports, Excel format, changing the report's columns.
First move: Now, 20 minutes: write a failing test that requests the export and checks
            the header row.
Assumed:    "Current report" means the filtered view on screen (changes Done). The
            existing report query can be reused (not checked).
```

## 7. Sales proposal (5-line card)

**Work card: proposal for the Quillmere Foods training request**

```
Outcome:    Quillmere Foods's head of operations has a priced proposal for a six-session
            training programme by Wednesday, in time for her Thursday budget meeting.
Done:       Scope, price and dates are on one page; the price matches the rate card;
            the client's three stated goals each map to a named session.
Out:        Custom materials, a second site, a pilot option.
First move: Today, 25 minutes: list the client's three stated goals from the email
            and put one session title beside each.
Assumed:    Thursday is a hard date (changes Outcome). Standard rate card applies (not
            checked).
```
