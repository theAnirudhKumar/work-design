# Habit methods: a short cheat-sheet

Evidence labels: Strong, Some, Mixed, Practice only. Be honest with the user: habit advice is popular, and much of it is better supported as common sense than as tested method. The plan is a bet, and the day-14 review is the test.

## Formation time varies widely

A well-known study of people forming a daily behaviour in real life found wide variation between individuals and between habits. There is no reliable "21 days" or "66 days". Do not state a fixed number to the user. **Evidence:** Some, for the claim that it varies; none for any specific figure.

## Starting small (the tiny version)

- **What:** Shrink the habit to something that takes under two minutes, so it can be done on the worst day.
- **Associated with:** BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits approach, among others.
- **Good for:** people who start big and quit; hard days.
- **Backfires when:** the tiny version becomes permanent with no scale-up, or when it is so small it feels pointless and is dropped.
- **Evidence:** Practice only. The logic is plausible, and direct tests of the small-start method are limited.

## The anchor, and if-then wording (implementation intentions)

- **What:** Tie the habit to an existing routine with "After I [routine], I will [habit]", or "If [cue], then [action]". Deciding in advance when and where removes the in-the-moment decision.
- **Comes from:** if-then planning is from research by Peter Gollwitzer; attaching a habit to an existing routine is popularly called habit stacking or anchoring.
- **Good for:** remembering to start; making a plan specific.
- **Backfires when:** the anchor is vague, irregular or does not happen every day.
- **Evidence:** Some. Studies of if-then planning for goals are broadly supportive in a range of settings; effects vary, and the anchored-habit version is less tested.

## Environment design (obvious cue, easy start)

- **What:** Change the physical setting so the cue is visible and the first step is easy; add friction to behaviours to do less of.
- **Good for:** starting; reducing temptation.
- **Backfires when:** the cue becomes wallpaper and stops being noticed (move or change it), or when the environment is shared and others object.
- **Evidence:** Some. Changes in convenience and visibility shift behaviour in a range of studies, though less is known about long-term habits.

## Tracking and streaks

- **What:** Mark each day the habit was done.
- **Good for:** a visible record; the satisfaction of a mark.
- **Backfires when:** a broken streak triggers "what the hell" abandonment. If a miss feels like a disaster, count days done this week instead of a streak.
- **Evidence:** Mixed. Self-monitoring generally supports behaviour change; whether streaks specifically help varies by person.

## The miss rule ("never miss twice")

- **What:** One missed day is expected. The rule is to do the tiny version the next day, with no catching up and no guilt.
- **Comes from:** a popular rule of thumb in the habit-building literature; no single origin to rely on.
- **Good for:** preventing a single miss from turning into quitting.
- **Backfires when:** it turns into a new source of guilt or an all-or-nothing rule. A longer break needs a restart plan, not shame.
- **Evidence:** Practice only.

## Temptation bundling

- **What:** Pair the habit with something enjoyable that you allow only then: a podcast only on walks, a favourite show only on the exercise bike.
- **Associated with:** Katy Milkman's research on the idea.
- **Good for:** dull habits.
- **Backfires when:** the enjoyable thing is used elsewhere, or the habit now depends on the reward.
- **Evidence:** Mixed. Some supportive studies; effects depend on the person and setting.

## One habit at a time

- **What:** Build one new habit, then add another.
- **Why:** each habit needs a cue, setup and attention; several at once raise the chance a slip becomes a collapse, and hide which part is working.
- **Backfires when:** the person has already automated most of a plan, or two habits naturally travel together (a morning stretch and a glass of water). Then two is fine.
- **Evidence:** Practice only. Reasonable, and not strongly tested.

## Scaling up

- **What:** Increase by one small notch only when the current version is easy; keep the tiny version for bad days; step back if it slips.
- **Backfires when:** done on a motivated week and then abandoned on a bad one.
- **Evidence:** Practice only.

## Health-adjacent notes

Habits touching sleep, eating, exercise or substances are general wellbeing practice here, not medical advice. For any health condition, injury, pregnancy, dependence or disordered pattern, follow a clinician's advice for that part of the plan.
