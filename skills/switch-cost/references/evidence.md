# Evidence: why this skill argues from mechanism, not a statistic

`stack-audit` cites Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index for what it measures: unused licences, spend per employee, spend growth. That index does not cover switching cost, migration hours or lock-in, and neither does anything else found while researching this skill.

Two sources were checked directly on 31 Aug 2026 while writing this skill:

- **Zylo's own 2026 SaaS statistics roundup** (the same publisher `stack-audit` already cites) contains no figures on vendor lock-in, data export difficulty, or migration time or cost. Checked directly; the gap is confirmed, not assumed.
- **A 2026 industry piece on enterprise AI vendor lock-in** makes claims of "tens of thousands of engineering hours" and "18 to 36 months" for specific migrations, with no cited source, no sample, and no methodology disclosed. Read as opinion and framework application, not measurement. Not usable as evidence, and not cited in the skill body.

No credible, sourced dataset on SaaS switching costs, across tools or within a category, was found anywhere in this pass. This mirrors the freelancer-scope-creep and onboarding-time-to-productivity findings from the 28 Aug work-design research (`Github Resources/2026-08-28-work-design-research-raw-extracts.md`, Brief 1 and Brief 2): some categories of workplace friction are widely discussed and never actually measured.

**What this means for the skill.** Step 4 builds an hours estimate from the specific inventory in Steps 1 through 3, and from the reader's own past migrations where one exists, rather than from a category-wide figure. That is a weaker-sounding claim than "switching costs the average company X hours." It is also the only claim here that survives being checked.

If a credible primary source on SaaS switching cost or data portability surfaces later, this file is where the correction goes.
