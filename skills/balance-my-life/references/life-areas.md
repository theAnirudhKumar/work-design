# Cheat-sheet: life areas, seasons and scripts

Short on purpose. Use it when drafting; do not recite it.

## Default areas and one prompt each

| Area | Ask yourself |
| :--- | :--- |
| Work or study | Is the time here chosen, or does it expand to fill space? |
| Health and body | Sleep, movement, food, check-ups: which one is slipping? |
| Family and partner | When did I last give undivided time? |
| Friends and community | Who would I like to see that I have not? |
| Money and life admin | What small admin, left alone, will cost me later? |
| Learning and growth | Is there something I want to get better at, apart from my job? |
| Rest and fun | When did I last do nothing useful and enjoy it? |
| Purpose (user names it) | Faith, craft, volunteering, a cause, a project of my own. |

Rename freely. A shift worker may want "sleep" as its own area; a student may merge work and learning.

## Seasons (balance is not an equal split)

A season is a stretch where one area legitimately takes more. Name it, give it an end or a review date, and name what is being protected at a minimum.

- New baby or caring role: sleep and partner time are the minimum; most else shrinks.
- Launch, busy season, deadline run: work grows for a set period; protect sleep and one anchor.
- Exams or thesis: study grows; keep movement and one social contact.
- Job search or change: work-like effort rises; keep routines that steady you.
- Recovery (illness, loss, move): everything shrinks; the shift should be small.

If a "season" has no end date and has run for a year, it is a pattern. Say that gently and ask whether it is chosen.

## Anchors

An anchor is a fixed, named slot for a personal commitment, placed before work. Give it a day, time, place or trigger, plus a floor version for a bad day (20 minutes, a phone call instead of a visit).

## Boundary script pattern

Fact, limit, offer. Soft and firm versions. Illustrative; names invented.

- To a boss: "I'm offline from 6:30 on weeknights for family dinner. Anything urgent, call me and I'll pick it up. Otherwise I'll answer first thing at 8:30." Firm: "I won't be answering messages after 6:30. For true emergencies, phone."
- To a client: "I reply within one working day. For anything that can't wait, tell me by 2pm and I'll plan it in."
- To a partner: "I've noticed I'm checking email at dinner, and I don't like it. I'd like to put the phone in the hall from 7 to 9. Can you hold me to it?"
- To family: "I can't make Sunday lunch every week. I can do the first Sunday of the month for the whole afternoon, and a phone call midweek."
- To a friend: "I've been bad at keeping in touch. Can we fix a regular coffee, every second Saturday?"

Keep scripts honest. Do not invent excuses. If the limit may affect a contract, visa, shift pattern or similar, say so and suggest the user check their own terms.

## Referral wording (say once, no diagnosis)

"What you've described sounds heavy, and I can't judge it from here. A doctor, a counsellor or a local helpline can help in ways I can't, and it's worth talking to one. If you'd like, I can carry on with the practical part."

If the user mentions immediate danger or self-harm: encourage contacting local emergency services or a crisis line now, and stop the exercise.
