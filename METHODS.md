# 137 productivity methods, with honest evidence ratings

Every method below has a full card with how to start in 10 minutes and a 7-day trial. Ratings are conservative: when the support is a popular practice rather than tested, it says so.

Evidence across the library: 4 Strong, 24 Some, 10 Mixed, 99 Practice only.

Not sure which to pick? Say "what productivity method should I use?" and `find-my-method` will choose one for you and set up a 7-day trial.

## Time and schedule

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pomodoro Technique** | Work on one task for 25 minutes with a timer, take a 5-minute break, and repeat; after four rounds take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes. | Some |
| **Flowtime** | Work until your focus naturally fades, note the start and stop times, then take a break proportional to how long you worked. | Practice only |
| **52/17 rule** | Work with full focus for 52 minutes, then rest for 17 minutes away from your desk, and repeat. | Practice only |
| **90-minute focus blocks** | Protect one or two uninterrupted blocks of about 90 minutes for your hardest work, with a real break of 15 to 20 minutes after each. | Practice only |
| **Time blocking** | Assign every part of your workday to a specific block on your calendar, including tasks, admin, meetings and breaks, then follow the calendar instead of a to-do list. | Some |
| **Timeboxing** | Give a task a fixed amount of time up front, and when the time is up you stop and review rather than keep going until it feels finished. | Practice only |
| **Task batching** | Group similar small tasks (email, calls, invoices, errands) and do them together in one session instead of scattered through the day. | Some |
| **Day theming** | Give each weekday a main focus (for example, Monday for planning, Tuesday for deep writing, Friday for admin) so that most days carry one type of work. | Practice only |
| **Ideal week** | Design a template week once, with recurring blocks for focus, meetings, admin, exercise and family, then plan each real week by adjusting that template. | Practice only |
| **Eat the frog** | Do your single most dreaded or most important task first thing, before email or anything easier. | Practice only |
| **Prime-time scheduling** | Work out when your energy and focus peak and place your hardest work there, leaving low-energy hours for routine tasks. | Some |
| **Parkinson's-law deadlines** | Set a shorter deadline on purpose so the work stops expanding to fill the time you gave it. | Mixed |

## Prioritisation

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eisenhower matrix** | Sort each task into one of four boxes by two yes/no questions, urgent and important: do now, schedule, delegate, or drop. | Practice only |
| **Ivy Lee method** | At the end of each day, write the six most important tasks for tomorrow in order, then work them one at a time from the top, moving unfinished ones to the next day's list. | Practice only |
| **1-3-5 rule** | Plan each day with exactly one big task, three medium tasks and five small tasks, nine in total. | Practice only |
| **Most Important Tasks** | Each day pick one to three Most Important Tasks (MITs) that would make the day a success if nothing else got done, and do them first. | Practice only |
| **Pareto principle** | Assume a small share of your tasks produces most of your results, find those few, and put your best effort there while trimming the rest. | Practice only |
| **ABCDE method** | Label every task A to E: A must do, B should do, C nice to do, D delegate, E eliminate; then number the A tasks A-1, A-2 and so on, and do A-1 first. | Practice only |
| **MoSCoW** | Sort items into Must have, Should have, Could have and Won't have (this time), so a team agrees what is in scope and what waits. | Practice only |
| **RICE scoring** | Score each option on Reach, Impact and Confidence, divide by Effort, and rank by the result. | Practice only |
| **Impact/effort matrix** | Plot each option on a two-by-two grid of impact (high or low) against effort (high or low) and start with high-impact, low-effort items. | Practice only |
| **Big rocks** | Schedule your few largest priorities first (the big rocks) so that the small tasks fill the gaps, rather than the reverse. | Practice only |
| **Hell yeah or no** | When asked to take on something new, accept only if your reaction is "hell yeah, I want to do that", and otherwise say no. | Practice only |
| **Not-to-do list** | Keep a written list of things you have decided to stop doing, so that your time is freed by subtraction as well as by planning. | Practice only |

## Capture and organise

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Getting Things Done** | Empty your head into one inbox, decide the next physical action for each item, and review everything weekly so you trust the lists. | Practice only |
| **PARA** | Sort everything you keep, notes and files alike, into four folders by how actionable it is: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archive. | Practice only |
| **Zettelkasten** | Write small notes, one idea each, in your own words, and link each to related notes so ideas build on one another. | Practice only |
| **Inbox zero** | Treat your inbox as a place to process mail, not store it: every message gets one decision and leaves. | Practice only |
| **Bullet Journal** | A paper notebook with a daily list, a monthly view and short symbols that mark tasks, events and notes, with unfinished items moved forward on purpose. | Practice only |
| **Personal Kanban** | Put your work on a board with columns like To do, Doing and Done, and cap how many items can be in Doing at once. | Practice only |
| **Personal Scrum** | Plan work in short fixed cycles, usually one week: pick a small set of tasks, do them, then look back on what worked. | Practice only |
| **Zen to Done** | A simplified relative of Getting Things Done that has you build ten habits one at a time, such as capture, process, plan and do. | Practice only |
| **Autofocus** | Keep one running list, scan it in order, and do whatever stands out; items you do not touch stay, items you work on get re-entered at the end. | Practice only |
| **Brain dump** | Write down everything on your mind, unsorted, in one sitting. | Some |
| **Someday/maybe list** | A parking list for things you might do one day, kept out of your active tasks and checked on a schedule. | Practice only |
| **Waiting-for list** | A list of everything you have handed to someone else or are waiting on, with the date and the person. | Practice only |

## Focus and attention

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deep work** | Schedule long blocks for demanding work with no interruptions, and treat them like appointments. | Practice only |
| **Monotasking** | Work on one thing at a time, close everything else, and finish or deliberately pause before switching. | Strong |
| **Notification batching** | Turn off most alerts and check messages at set times, so they come to you in groups instead of one at a time. | Some |
| **Phone-away rule** | During focus time, the phone is out of sight and out of arm's reach, not just face down. | Mixed |
| **Website blocking** | Use a blocker app or browser extension to make distracting sites unreachable during set hours. | Practice only |
| **Distraction parking lot** | Keep a scrap of paper beside you, and write down any urge or stray thought instead of acting on it. | Practice only |
| **Body doubling** | Work alongside another person, in the room or on a video call, each doing your own task. | Practice only |
| **Ulysses contract** | Make a commitment ahead of time that takes away your ability to give in later, such as handing over passwords or paying a penalty. | Mixed |
| **Email batching** | Read and answer email in a few set slots per day instead of keeping it open. | Mixed |
| **Meeting-free blocks** | Reserve fixed times in the week, such as mornings or one whole day, when no meetings may be booked. | Practice only |
| **Maker's schedule** | If your work needs long stretches to create, divide the week into large blocks, with meetings bunched together rather than scattered. | Practice only |
| **Shutdown ritual** | End the workday with a short fixed routine, then stop working until the next day. | Some |

## Starting and procrastination

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Two-minute rule** | If a task takes under two minutes, do it now; if you are avoiding a bigger one, agree to work on it for just two minutes. | Practice only |
| **Five-minute rule** | Work on the task for five minutes, then decide whether to stop or continue. | Practice only |
| **Swiss cheese method** | Poke small holes in a big task by doing short, unordered bites of it in spare moments, so the whole thing stops looking solid. | Practice only |
| **Temptation bundling** | Pair something you should do but dread with something you enjoy, and allow the enjoyable thing only during the dreaded one. | Mixed |
| **Implementation intentions** | Decide in advance exactly when, where and how you will act, written as "If [situation], then I will [action]." | Strong |
| **Commitment devices** | Set up a consequence or restriction now that makes the avoided behaviour easier to do, or the avoidance harder, later. | Some |
| **Ugly first draft** | Produce a deliberately rough version fast, with permission for it to be bad, then improve it. | Practice only |
| **Tiny first step** | Shrink the start of a task until it is so small that refusing feels silly, such as opening the document or putting on your shoes. | Practice only |
| **Don't break the chain** | Mark each day you do the task on a calendar and try not to leave a gap in the row of marks. | Practice only |
| **Accountability partner** | Tell another person what you will do and by when, and report back to them. | Some |

## Habits and routines

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tiny Habits** | Pick a behaviour so small it takes under a minute, tie it to an existing moment, and celebrate it right after. | Practice only |
| **Habit stacking** | Attach a new habit to a specific habit you already do, in the form "After I [current habit], I will [new habit]." | Practice only |
| **Two-day rule** | Never skip a habit two days in a row; one miss is allowed, a second consecutive one is not. | Practice only |
| **Environment design** | Change your surroundings so the habit is easy to do and the competing behaviour is harder. | Some |
| **Keystone habits** | Focus on one habit that tends to pull other good habits along with it, such as regular exercise, a weekly review or a fixed bedtime. | Practice only |
| **Minimum viable habit** | Define the smallest version of your habit that still counts, and do it on days when the full version is impossible. | Practice only |
| **Habit tracking** | Record each time you do a habit, so you can see how often you do it and adjust. | Some |
| **Morning routine** | A short, fixed sequence of actions you do after waking, so the start of the day does not rely on decisions. | Practice only |
| **Evening routine** | A short sequence at the end of the day that closes your work, tidies up for tomorrow and winds you down. | Practice only |
| **Identity-based habits** | Frame a habit as something a person like you does ("I am someone who moves daily"), then cast small votes for that identity. | Practice only |

## Goals and planning

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **OKRs** | Write one qualitative objective, then a few measurable key results that show whether you got there, and review them on a fixed cycle. | Practice only |
| **SMART goals** | Rewrite a goal so it is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and has a deadline. | Some |
| **WOOP** | Name a wish, picture the best outcome, name the main inner obstacle, then write an if-then plan for meeting it. | Some |
| **Working backwards** | Start from the finished result, then step back through what had to be true just before it, until you reach what to do today. | Practice only |
| **Annual theme** | Choose one word or short phrase to steer the year, in place of a list of resolutions. | Practice only |
| **12 Week Year** | Treat 12 weeks as the whole year: set a few goals, plan them week by week, and score your execution every week. | Practice only |
| **Wheel of Life** | Rate your satisfaction in eight or so life areas on a circle, then look at which areas are lopsided. | Practice only |
| **Goal ladder** | Link a long-term aim down through yearly, monthly and weekly steps so today's task connects to something you care about. | Practice only |
| **Anti-goals** | Write down what you refuse to optimise, avoid or become, so your goals have limits. | Practice only |
| **Milestone mapping** | Split a long project into three to seven checkpoints, each with a date and a visible "done" test. | Practice only |

## Decisions

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **10-10-10 rule** | Ask how you will feel about each option in 10 minutes, 10 months and 10 years. | Practice only |
| **Regret minimization** | Imagine yourself at 80 and ask which choice you would regret more, taking it or not taking it. | Practice only |
| **Weighted decision matrix** | Score each option against criteria you have weighted by importance, then compare totals. | Some |
| **Decision journal** | Write down each important decision, your reasoning and expectations at the time, then review the outcome later. | Practice only |
| **Sleep-on-it rule** | For decisions that are not urgent, wait a night before committing. | Mixed |
| **Two-way door decisions** | Sort decisions into reversible (two-way door) and hard to reverse (one-way door), and decide fast on the first kind. | Practice only |
| **Inversion** | Instead of asking how to succeed, ask how you would fail or what to avoid, then do the opposite. | Practice only |
| **Satisficing** | Decide what is good enough before you look, then pick the first option that meets it instead of hunting for the best. | Mixed |

| **Micro-breaks** | Take very short pauses of under five minutes between or within tasks, away from the screen or the task. | Some |

## Review and reflection

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Daily review** | Spend five to ten minutes at the end of the day to close loops and set up tomorrow. | Practice only |
| **Weekly review** | Once a week, clear your inbox, look at last week and next week, and choose your focus. | Practice only |
| **Monthly review** | Once a month, step back to check whether your goals, habits and commitments still match what you want. | Practice only |
| **Annual review** | Once a year, look back at the whole year and decide what to keep, stop and start. | Practice only |
| **Personal retrospective** | Look back on a project, a quarter or a rough patch with a short structure: what happened, why, and what to change. | Some |
| **Start-stop-continue** | List what to start, stop and continue doing, then pick one from each. | Practice only |
| **Wins log** | Write down what went well, even small things, as they happen or at the end of each day. | Some |
| **Time audit** | Record where your time actually goes for a few days, then compare it with where you think it goes. | Practice only |
| **Morning pages** | Write three pages of longhand, stream-of-consciousness, first thing in the morning, without editing. | Practice only |

## Learning and knowledge

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spaced repetition** | Review each item at growing gaps (a day, a few days, a week, a month) instead of cramming it once. | Strong |
| **Active recall** | Pull the answer out of your head before checking it, instead of re-reading. | Strong |
| **Feynman technique** | Explain the idea in plain words as if teaching a beginner, and study wherever your explanation breaks down. | Some |
| **Interleaving** | Mix different problem types or topics within one session instead of finishing one block before starting the next. | Some |
| **SQ3R** | A five-step reading routine: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. | Some |
| **Cornell notes** | Split each page into a notes column, a cue column, and a summary strip, and use the cues to quiz yourself later. | Practice only |
| **Deliberate practice** | Train a specific weak part of a skill, at the edge of your ability, with fast feedback, instead of repeating what you can already do. | Mixed |
| **Learning in public** | Share what you are learning as you go (notes, drafts, questions, small projects) so others can see and respond. | Practice only |

## Working with others

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Async-first** | Default to written updates and decisions that people answer when ready, and reserve live calls for what truly needs them. | Practice only |
| **Meeting-free days** | Reserve one or more weekdays with no internal meetings so people get unbroken work time. | Practice only |
| **Daily standup** | A short, regular check-in where each person says what they are doing and where they need help. | Practice only |
| **RACI** | For one piece of work, name who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed. | Practice only |
| **Decision log** | Keep a simple running list of decisions made, with the date, owner, reason, and what was ruled out. | Practice only |
| **Directly responsible individual** | Every task or project has exactly one named person who is answerable for it, even if many people help. | Practice only |
| **Working agreements** | A short list of rules the team writes together about how it communicates, meets, and hands work over. | Practice only |
| **Delegation levels** | Choose how much authority to hand over, from "do as I say" up to full delegation, and name the level out loud. | Practice only |

## Managing a team

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **One-on-ones** | A regular private meeting between a lead and one person, where the agenda belongs mainly to that person. | Practice only |
| **Weekly team planning** | A short fixed meeting at the start of the week where the team agrees its few priorities and who owns each. | Practice only |
| **Team retrospective** | A regular meeting where the team looks back on how it worked, picks one or two changes, and tries them. | Practice only |
| **WIP limits** | Cap how many items can be in progress at once, so people finish work before starting more. | Practice only |
| **Team Kanban board** | A shared board of columns showing every piece of team work and where it stands, from waiting to done. | Practice only |
| **Situational leadership** | Match how directive or supportive you are to the person's skill and confidence on this particular task, not to the person in general. | Practice only |
| **Radical candor** | Give feedback that is both direct and caring, so people hear it and act on it. | Practice only |
| **Skip-level meetings** | Occasional meetings between a leader and people who report to their direct reports, to hear how things look further down. | Practice only |
| **Written weekly updates** | Each person or team sends a short written update once a week, covering progress, plans, and problems, and the lead reads them all. | Practice only |
| **Team pre-mortem** | Before a project starts, the team imagines it has failed and writes down why, then acts on the likeliest causes. | Practice only |

## Personal life and admin

| Method | What it is | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Life admin hour** | One fixed weekly hour for forms, bills, appointments, and other small chores, so they stop leaking into the rest of the week. | Practice only |
| **Errand batching** | Group errands by location and route into one or two trips instead of many small ones. | Practice only |
| **Meal prep batching** | Cook or prepare parts of several meals in one session so weeknights need only assembly or reheating. | Practice only |
| **Family planning meeting** | A short regular meeting where a household looks at the week ahead, shares the load, and settles clashes before they happen. | Practice only |
| **Digital declutter** | Spend a short session clearing what clutters your devices, then set one rule to keep it from refilling. | Practice only |
| **One in one out** | When something new comes in, something similar goes out, so a category never grows. | Practice only |
| **Budget check-in** | A short weekly habit of looking at your own money-in and money-out numbers so nothing surprises you. This is a habit of looking, not financial advice. | Practice only |
| **Weekly time together** | A protected slot each week for a partner, family, or friends, put in the calendar like any other commitment. | Practice only |
